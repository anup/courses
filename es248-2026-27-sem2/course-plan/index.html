Course Plan

ES248: Introduction to DSA in C Programming. You will write C programs every week, starting from the basics. If you have never coded before, the course starts where you are.

SYS

You need Linux

Install Ubuntu on your laptop before the first class. A MacBook also works, since macOS already runs Unix commands. We compile with gcc in the terminal.

Week 1: the baseline test

In the first week everyone takes a short coding test. It carries no marks. It tells us how much C you already know, so we can teach you at the right pace.

A

Foundation group

For students who are new to coding or scored low on the baseline test. We start from the very beginning: what a program is, variables, printf and scanf, conditions and loops. Foundation sessions are extra to the regular classes, and attendance is required. When you can solve the weekly contest problems on your own, you move to the core group.

B

Core group

For students who can already write simple C programs. You attend the regular classes and are expected to solve more of each weekly contest.

Both groups follow the same topics, take the same exams and are graded the same way. The foundation group gets extra time and help at the start.

Topics

The same topics as last semester, with more time for graph search at the end.

Before the mid-semester exam

  1. Linux basics — moving around the filesystem, running commands, compiling with gcc.
  2. C basics — data types, variables, input and output, operators, conditions.
  3. Loops — for, while, and nested loops.
  4. Arrays — how arrays sit in memory, passing them to functions, inserting, deleting and simple sorting.
  5. Strings — characters and ASCII, the null terminator, scanf, and strlen, strcpy, strcat, strcmp.
  6. Pointers, part I — addresses, pointer arithmetic, pointers and arrays, call by reference.
  7. Endianness — how multi-byte values are stored in memory.

After the mid-semester exam

  1. Pointers, parts II and III — 2D arrays, malloc and free, function pointers.
  2. Structures and unions
  3. Time complexity — counting steps and Big O.
  4. Linked lists
  5. Stacks
  6. Queues
  7. BFS, DFS and backtracking — searching grids and graphs. Two weeks this time, not one.

Last semester's slides for each topic are on the previous offering's page. Updated slides will be posted as we reach each topic.

Grading

Two exams, a weekly contest, regular practice and attendance. Total 100 marks.

25

Mid-semester exam

Held in the lab. Part A is multiple-choice questions; Part B is coding problems in C.

30

End-semester exam

Same format as the mid-semester exam. It covers the whole course.

25

Weekly contest

Every week, take part in one rated contest: CodeChef Starters (Wednesday, 8:00–10:00 PM) or the AtCoder Beginner Contest (usually Saturday, 5:30 PM). Starts in week 2. Your three weakest weeks are dropped. Scoring is below.

10

Regular practice

Take part in more contests on CodeChef, AtCoder, LeetCode or Codeforces. You get 1 mark for each extra contest where you solve at least one problem in C, up to 10 marks. The contest you use for your weekly mark does not count again here.

10

Attendance

Marks for attending your classes. If you are in the foundation group, this includes the foundation sessions.

How each weekly contest is scored

Each week is scored out of 10, by the number of problems you solve in C during the contest. Your rating, rank and division do not matter.

  • Take part but solve nothing: 2
  • Solve 1 problem: 4
  • Solve 2 problems: 7
  • Solve 3 problems: 9
  • Solve 4 or more: 10

If you take both contests in a week, the better one counts. Your weekly contest mark is the average of your best weeks (all weeks except your three weakest), scaled to 25.

Use the username you registered. We track your contests automatically using the CodeChef and AtCoder usernames you submit in the first week. Contests on any other account do not count.

Rules

  • Only solutions written in C count, for both the weekly contest and regular practice.
  • You may be asked to explain any solution you submitted in a short viva. If you cannot explain it, it does not count.
  • Do the work yourself. Copied or insincere work can lose marks.
