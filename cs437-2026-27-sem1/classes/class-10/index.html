3. The machine is shared. Find out what that means.

Twenty-eight of you are on one machine with one public address. Run these and work out what each one is telling you. Two of them explain something that will otherwise cost you an hour later.

w                                  # who else is logged in
ss -lun                            # every nameserver on the box, including theirs
ps aux | grep python               # you can see their processes
cat /home/raj/solve.py             # but not their files. why?
ls -ld /home/raj ~                 # read the first ten characters
free -h; nproc; uptime             # one core, 2 GB, 28 of you

Two more, once something of yours is running:

  • Can you ask a classmate's nameserver a question from this machine? Their ports are in the table above.
  • Try binding port 53, then port 8053. Both fail. The reasons are completely different. Work out what they are — this is the one that saves you an hour later.

4. Prove your port is reachable before you touch the challenge

Two things have to be true, and it is worth establishing them in that order:

  • something you started is listening on your port, on the server
  • a query sent from your own laptop actually reaches it

You are going to write a nameserver anyway. Get the simplest possible one answering on your port first, then prove from your own machine that you can reach it. dnslib is already installed.

Hint: dig is the usual way to ask a nameserver a question directly, and it takes both a server and a port. Windows does not have dig — find the equivalent.

If you cannot get an answer, stop and fix that. Nothing else will work until you can, and a dropped packet looks exactly like a server that is not running.

5. The challenge

Serving DNS — 60 points.

export HACKATTIC_TOKEN=your_own_token     # never commit this

Submit:

{"dns_ip": "204.168.202.138", "dns_port": YOUR_PORT}

You have 60 seconds from fetching the problem. Your server must already be running and answering before you fetch. Structure it: listen, then fetch, then load the records, then submit. Rejected attempts cost nothing — retry as often as you need.
