Your code review

For SAAB, git_happens, MADHAM, Last_3_Brain_Cells, TriMind and DevFlow. Open your hackathon repo’s Issues tab and filter by the label code-review.

  1. a

    Read each issue and decide

    Start with the ones marked [Critical] and [High]. Use the “How to see it” steps to check the problem really happens in your app. A reviewer can be wrong.

    DecideReal → fix it (b). Not real, or not worth fixing now → reply on the issue saying why, with evidence (a test, a log, a line of code). A reasoned reply counts as much as a fix.
  2. b

    Fix it the hackathon way

    Write the test the issue suggests and watch it fail. Then fix the code until it passes. Then a teammate reviews the pull request.

    ProofA pull request that says Fixes #<issue number>, so the issue closes itself when it merges.
  3. c

    Share out the rest

    Assign each remaining issue to one member on GitHub, so everyone owns a part. The “Smaller things” checklist can be ticked off one box at a time.

    By 16 NovEvery Critical and High issue closed or answered, and the rest assigned.

Prof. Neeldhara Misra visits today

She will go round the groups during the work time and try your app as a user, then give feedback on its design and how easy it is to use.

  1. 1

    Be ready before she reaches you

    Have the live app open on a phone (and a laptop), signed out, on its first screen. Pick the one thing your user comes to do, and be ready to say it in one sentence.

    Not live yet?Show it running on a laptop; say what is blocking deployment.
  2. 2

    Let her drive

    Hand her the phone and ask her to do that one thing. Don’t explain or point unless she asks. Where she hesitates, gets stuck or asks a question is the feedback. One person writes it down word for word.

    Then ask“What was confusing?” and “What would you change first?”
  3. 3

    Turn it into issues, and fix one

    Each point becomes a GitHub issue labelled usability, with what she did and what she said. Pick the one that would most help a first-time user and fix it before you leave, through a reviewed pull request.

    ProofThe usability issues, and one of them closed today.

The five levels

Each level has a proof. A level counts only when its proof exists.

  1. 1

    Live

    Your app is online at a link anyone on campus can open on a phone, over HTTPS.

    ProofThe link, posted in the InsIIT integration chat.
  2. 2

    Ships itself

    Every merge to main updates the live site automatically, after the tests pass. The README says where it runs and how a deploy happens.

    ProofThe deploy run (GitHub Actions or your host’s log) for your latest merge.
  3. 3

    Fixed

    Every Critical and High issue from your code review is fixed or answered, as described above.

    ProofThe code-review issues: closed by pull requests with their new tests, or answered with reasons.
  4. 4

    Used

    At least five people outside your group have used the live app for its real purpose (Prof. Neeldhara counts as one). You noted what confused them and fixed one thing.

    ProofUSERS.md in your repo: who (first name or role), when, what they did, what confused them, what you changed.
  5. 5

    Ready for 16 November

    Another group asks for a small new feature in one sentence. You add it to SPEC.md, write a failing test, build it, get it reviewed and merged, and it is live, all within 30 minutes.

    ProofThe pull request, with the request quoted at the top and the time it took.
