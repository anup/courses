Start from the other team's main as it is now. Your own lift is frozen: push nothing more to your own main. Another team is building on it.

2. First 15 minutes: get their lift running

  1. Clone their group repo. If GitHub refuses, their team has not added you as a collaborator yet: tell them, now.
  2. Follow only their README. Replay one of their scenarios, run their test command, open their UI.
  3. If it does not run from the README after 15 minutes, tell an instructor. We note it; you carry on with whatever runs.

3. The three changes

Every team makes all three. They get harder from 1 to 3. Split them in your team: one change per person. Tell an instructor who owns which in the first 15 minutes.

Who takes which: whoever built most of your own team's lift yesterday takes Fire mode, and reviews the other two pull requests. If you wrote little or none of your team's lift, today is where you do your share: take Basement or Destination dispatch.

4. Make the changes

  1. You work in the other team's group repo: the team whose lift you are changing (from section 1). Each person makes their own branch there, named after your team and your change. For example, G3 changes G4's lift, so in G4's repo G3 makes three branches: G3-fire, G3-basement and G3-dispatch.
  2. Change their lift so it does exactly what your change says. The seven rules must still hold everywhere else.
  3. Add at least two scenarios that use your change to elevator/scenarios/. Put one line saying what each tests in a "description" field in the scenario file.
  4. Add a test for your change, so their one test command checks it.
  5. Add a paragraph to their README: what you changed, and how to try it.
  6. Open a pull request from your branch into the other team's main, titled with the change and your team, for example Fire mode (from G3). One pull request per change. A teammate from your team reviews and approves it. Do not merge it. We score your branch as it is at the deadline.

Each branch starts from the other team's main and carries only its own change. The three are never merged together, so they are scored separately and cannot clash.

5. Found a bug in their lift?

This is the bonus from section 13 of the Class 12 page. You can hunt only in the lift your team is changing, since that is the only other repo you can open. It runs until Sunday 4 October, 23:59.

  1. Write a scenario that makes their lift break one of the seven rules.
  2. Open an issue in their repo, titled Bug: rule N. Paste the scenario and one line on what goes wrong.
  3. We run your scenario through our checker. It counts only if the rule really breaks. GitHub's timestamp settles who found it first.

Do not fix it in your change branch unless it stops your change from working. If you do, say so in your pull request.

6. Before you leave

In your own repo, lab-notes/class-13.md, in "I": which part you did, what in their code helped you, and what got in your way.

7. How it is marked

Due Wednesday 30 September, 23:59. We score each pull request branch as it is then.

As announced in section 12 of the Class 12 page, your change is marked on three things, for each of the three changes:

  • It works: we run check scenarios of our own for that change.
  • Nothing broken: the seven rules still hold on the changed lift. Only breaks your change introduced count against you. If the lift you received already broke a rule, that is not yours.
  • Your test and README paragraph.

Each change has one owner, so everyone in your team does a real part of the work. The other team's own marks come from their frozen lift; nothing you do today changes them.
