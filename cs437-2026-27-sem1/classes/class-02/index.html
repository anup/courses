What's the objective of this course?

You will learn to be responsible
for code you did not write.

The AI does the typing from day one — that part needs no course.

By December

Someone who cannot code asks your team for a new feature — in plain English.

Your system, not you, builds it and proves it works.

That's the final test. Everything between now and then is preparation for it.

The entry bar

Describe what you want in plain English. That's the only prerequisite.

One shared stack

We all build web apps, on one framework we learn together. Same ground for everyone.

The stack isn't the point

You're learning to direct AI and own what it builds — that transfers anywhere.

It typed the whole thing.
So what's left for you to learn?

Say it precisely

Know what to ask for.

Check it's right

Know whether the answer is correct — and safe.

Own it

Explain it, fix it, defend it.

Those three are the syllabus.

The semester, in plain words

  • Month 1 — learn to direct the AI safely. It starts with Git: your undo button for everything.
  • Month 2 — your team builds a real product and puts it on the internet.
  • Month 3 — you attack your own product: security, testing, load. AI-built software fails in predictable ways.
  • Final week — the plain-English feature test.

The one rule

You may use AI for everything.
You may hide behind it for nothing.

If you submit it, you can explain it, test it, and defend it.

Your AI log in the repo is where you show your thinking. It's graded as evidence of understanding — not of effort.
