The Hackattic Contest

Yesterday was the free run. Today is scored. Ninety minutes, four challenges, and your group is measured on what it actually clears — with one catch: a challenge nobody in your group can explain does not count.

Before anything else — can everyone log in?

Five people do not have a hackattic account yet. If you are one of them, fix it in the first two minutes: go to hackattic.com, choose Log in with GitHub, done. Your hackattic username becomes your GitHub username.

This matters more than it sounds. Your problem data is generated per account. A member without an account cannot contribute a single point, and one group currently has only one member who can play. Sort it before the clock starts.

One challenge today needs a library. Run this now, not at minute forty:

pip install cryptography

Notice the shape of this. Redis alone is worth more than the other three combined, because it is harder than the other three combined. Three easy clears is a respectable score. One Redis clear beats it. Decide early how ambitious you are, because you cannot do both in ninety minutes.

How scoring works

  1. Your group's score is the sum of the points of the distinct challenges it clears.
  2. A challenge counts once, however many of you clear it. Two people solving Mini miner scores 15, not 30. Split up.
  3. Any member's account counts for the group. It does not matter who clears what.
  4. Ties are broken by how many different members cleared something. If two groups finish on 55 points, the group where all three contributed beats the group where one person did everything.

Rule 4 exists for a reason and it is not decoration. One strong member can carry the raw score. They cannot carry the tiebreak, and they cannot carry the viva.

How to claim a challenge

Nothing counts unless an instructor saw it during the session. No screenshots afterwards, no "we definitely solved it", no claims after the clock stops.

  1. Your submission comes back accepted on hackattic.
  2. Raise your hand. An instructor comes over and looks at your screen.
  3. The instructor picks one member of your group — not the one who solved it, and not one you choose — and asks three questions.
  4. If that member answers them, the points go on the board.

The three questions, in advance

There is no trick here. These are the questions, you know them now, and they are the same for every challenge:

  1. What did the problem give you? What was in the JSON when you fetched it.
  2. What did you compute? In plain words — the actual step, not "we used a library".
  3. What did you send back? The field names and what was in them.

Sixty seconds, no code reading, no memorised line numbers. If you understand what your group did, you will pass this without preparing for it.

If the answer does not come

The challenge does not count — but you may claim it once more, later in the session. Go and understand it together first, then put your hand up again. This is not a trap and it is not a single shot.

Which tells you the winning strategy outright: the moment someone clears a challenge, they should spend two minutes explaining it to the other two. That is cheaper than losing the points, and it is the actual point of the exercise.

The rules, complete

1You compete as your group. Nine groups.
2Everyone solves on their own hackattic account. Your problem data is generated for you — your teammate's answer is literally wrong for you, so there is nothing to copy.
3Any AI, any model, any tool. No restrictions, and we are not checking. The viva is the check, and a model that one-shots a challenge you cannot explain has cost you the points rather than won them.
4Talk within your group. Not across groups.
5Rejected submissions cost nothing. Retry as often as you like. Only claims are gated, and a rejection is not a claim.
6You may attempt the challenges in any order, and you may abandon one and come back to it.
7If hackattic errors or rate-limits you, tell an instructor at the time. We will note it. Do not discover it afterwards and ask for points.
8The board closes at the announced time. A submission accepted at the buzzer still needs its viva, so leave yourself the minute.

“Can I use Claude / Copilot / Gemini / anything?” Yes. Rule 3.
