1. Prof. Neeldhara’s feedback

She used your live app as a student would. Her points are now issues in your repo, labelled usability.

  1. a

    Fix first

    Issues marked [Fix]: something a first-time user gets stuck on or misreads. Fix these first, each through a reviewed pull request that says Fixes #<number>.

  2. b

    Ideas are optional

    Issues marked [Idea] are larger suggestions and optional. An idea that makes the app genuinely better for its users, built well and finished, earns more marks; a half-built one earns none. If you skip one, reply on the issue saying why.

  3. c

    The sooner, the better

    Close everything before 16 November. Each time you close a batch, tell us in the InsIIT chat: we look at your app again and send fresh feedback, so an early start means more rounds and a better app.

2. Swap and test (30 minutes)

Each group tries another group’s live app on their phones, as a real user would.

Pairs

SeshX ↔ CabShare · Dhulai ↔ Palaj Bazaar · CoursePath ↔ Lost & Found · Campus Recall, Anything and TreeGPT test each other.

How

The owners say one sentence about what the app is for, then stay quiet. The testers try to do that one thing. Owners write down where they hesitate or get stuck, word for word.

Then

Owners turn each point into a usability issue in their own repo, starting the issue with “Reported by <tester’s name>”.

Why it is worth doing. Testers: every problem you report that the owners accept (a usability issue that names you) counts towards your own hackathon marks. Owners: testers from other groups count as real users of your app.

3. Code review again, after your fixes

Once your usability fixes are in, we will review your code again and look at the app as a whole. Anything new arrives as a code-review issue, the same as before. Keep closing issues the hackathon way: failing test, fix, teammate’s review.
