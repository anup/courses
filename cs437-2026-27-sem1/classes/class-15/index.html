Class 15 · Tuesday 6 October

Prove Your User Exists

Eight groups posted an idea last night. None of them named a real person, and none said what a user told them. Today you fix that, then turn what your user says into your first five spec statements.

1. Test your idea (20 minutes)

Answer these as a group, in writing.

  1. Who, exactly? A name and a role: "<name>, supervisor at the main-gate security cabin", not "the IITGN community". One person you can talk to this week.
  2. The last time. When did this person last do the job your software helps with? Walk through what they actually did, step by step.
  3. What it costs them. How often does it happen, and what goes wrong: time lost, money lost, something missed?
  4. What already exists. Is there a portal, an app, a WhatsApp group, a spreadsheet that does this? What does yours do that it does not?
  5. Who has to say yes. Do you need someone's data or permission to make it real: an office, a committee, a website that needs a login? Ask them this week.
  6. Where it grows. Write three features your user might ask for next. On 16 November, someone will ask for one you have not seen.

2. Talk to your user (this week)

If your user is on campus, go now: during class counts. Otherwise book a time today. Ask about what they do now, not about your app. "Tell me about the last time you…" gets you facts. "Would you use an app that…?" gets you a polite yes.

Write it down at the top of your SPEC.md, in a section called ## Our user:

## Our user
Who:       name, role, how you know them
When/how:  date; in person, call or chat; who from the group was there
They said: "two or three things, in their own words"
Changed:   one thing in our idea that changed because of what they said
Evidence:  docs/user/ — a photo, a screenshot of the chat, or notes they agreed to

At least one conversation; two is better.

3. Draft five spec statements (30 minutes)

Each statement says one thing your user can do, or one thing that must never happen, so that a test can check it. Write them from what your user told you, not from features you like.

WeakCheckable
Signing up is easy.A student can register for an event from its page in one step and sees "You are registered".
Events don't overfill.When all 40 seats are taken, the next student who registers goes on the waitlist and is not registered.
It is private.A student never sees another student's phone number on the sign-up list.
Organisers manage things.Only the club coordinator can cancel an event, and every registered student is notified when it is cancelled.

For each of your five:

  1. Under it, one line: what would a test do, and what would it look for?
  2. Say which user quote it comes from.
  3. At least one of the five says what must never happen.

If you use an AI model inside your app, write the statement about what your code controls: which document is cited, whether it refuses when unsure. Do not write it about the exact wording of an answer, which a test cannot pin down.

4. Before you leave

  1. One person replies to your group's idea in the CS437 space with: your user's name and role, and when you will talk to them (or what they said, if you already have). G2: post your idea in three sentences first. G5: post the narrowed version.
  2. In your own repo, lab-notes/class-15.md, in "I": which question in section 1 your group could not answer, and the statement you wrote.
